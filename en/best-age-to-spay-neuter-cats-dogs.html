What is the best age to spay or neuter a cat or dog?

For cats, major feline organizations support neutering by about five months of age when appropriate. For dogs, evidence increasingly supports an individualized approach rather than one universal age for every breed and sex.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Species, sex, expected adult size, breed-associated orthopedic and cancer risk, behavior, reproductive risk, and the ability to prevent accidental breeding all belong in the decision.

Cats: why the five-month target?

Cats can become reproductively active surprisingly young. FelineVMA/AAFP-supported initiatives recommend sterilization by five months for cats not intended for breeding, helping prevent unplanned litters before the first heat in many females.

Small and large dogs are not identical

AAHA life-stage guidance has historically supported earlier neutering for many small dogs while allowing later timing for larger dogs after more skeletal growth. Breed-specific observational studies have found different associations between age at neutering and some orthopedic or cancer outcomes.

What the evidence cannot do

Most timing studies are observational and cannot prove that one age directly causes or prevents a later disorder in every dog. Breed, genetics, body condition, activity, and healthcare also influence outcomes.

Questions to discuss with your veterinarian

What is the expected adult weight? Can intact males and females be reliably separated? Are there breed-specific orthopedic concerns? Is there a medical reason to operate sooner? For females, how do pregnancy prevention, pyometra risk, mammary-tumor considerations, and potential urinary effects fit the individual case?
