What are the black spots on my cat's chin?

Small black comedones around the chin and lower lip are commonly associated with feline acne. Mild cases may be mostly cosmetic, while inflamed or infected cases can become painful.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Do not squeeze the blackheads. Redness, swelling, crusting, pus, or pain means the skin needs veterinary assessment.

What causes feline acne?

The exact cause is not always clear. Abnormal keratinization, oil production, grooming, local irritation, stress, and secondary bacterial infection may contribute. Plastic bowls are sometimes suspected because scratches can harbor debris, but switching bowls is not a cure for every cat.

What does it look like?

Mild disease often appears as pepper-like black comedones. More advanced lesions can include redness, swelling, pustules, crusts, hair loss, or draining infection.

How is it treated?

Treatment depends on severity and may include gentle cleansing, topical veterinary antiseptics, treatment of secondary infection, and investigation for mites or fungal disease when the appearance is atypical.

What should I avoid?

Do not use acne products made for people, essential oils, concentrated peroxide, or aggressive scrubbing. These can irritate feline skin or be unsafe if licked.

Keep bowls clean

Wash food and water bowls regularly and monitor whether local irritation improves.
