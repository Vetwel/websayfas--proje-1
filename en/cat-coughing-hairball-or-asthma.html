My cat is coughing. Is it a hairball or asthma?

A coughing cat may crouch low, extend the neck, and make repeated expiratory efforts. Because this can look like retching, owners often assume every episode is a hairball.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

If no hairball is produced and episodes recur, respiratory disease—including feline asthma—should be considered.

Emergency: open-mouth breathing, blue or gray gums, severe abdominal effort, collapse, or inability to settle requires immediate veterinary care.

Coughing versus vomiting

Vomiting is usually associated with nausea and abdominal contractions and may produce food or fluid. Coughing is a respiratory reflex and often produces nothing. A video of an episode is extremely useful.

What is feline asthma?

Feline asthma is an inflammatory lower-airway disease associated with bronchoconstriction and mucus. Cats may cough, wheeze, breathe faster, or have episodic respiratory distress. Other diseases can mimic asthma, so diagnosis requires clinical evaluation.

How is coughing investigated?

Chest radiographs, parasite assessment, blood tests, airway sampling, or other testing may be considered depending on severity and history. Heart disease, infection, lungworms, and other conditions can produce respiratory signs.

Environmental support

Reduce smoke, aerosols, dusty litter, strong fragrances, and other respiratory irritants. Do not use human inhalers or steroids unless prescribed for the cat with an appropriate delivery plan.
