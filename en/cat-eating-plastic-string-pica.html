Why does my cat eat plastic or string?

Pica is the repeated chewing or ingestion of non-food material. It can be behavioral, compulsive, exploratory, or associated with medical problems—and swallowing string can become a surgical emergency.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

If string is hanging from the mouth or anus, do not pull it. A linear foreign body can be anchored internally and can damage the intestine.

Urgent signs: vomiting, repeated retching, abdominal pain, refusal to eat, lethargy, drooling, or known swallowing of thread, ribbon, yarn, dental floss, or string warrants rapid veterinary advice.

Why do cats chew non-food objects?

Play and oral exploration are common in young cats. Persistent pica can also occur with stress, under-stimulation, compulsive behavior, gastrointestinal disease, anemia, or other medical problems. A nutritional deficiency should not be assumed without evidence.

Why string is dangerous

Linear objects can lodge under the tongue or in the stomach while the intestine attempts to move the free end. This can cause the bowel to bunch and potentially cut through intestinal tissue.

How can the home be made safer?

Secure sewing supplies, ribbon, hair ties, rubber bands, plastic bags, cords, and string toys when unsupervised. Provide safe play, food puzzles, chewing alternatives appropriate to cats, and predictable routines.

When behavior treatment is needed

If medical disease and foreign-body risk are addressed but pica persists, a structured environmental and behavior plan may be needed. Punishment can increase stress without eliminating the motivation to chew.
