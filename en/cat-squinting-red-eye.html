My cat is squinting and the eye is red. What could it be?

Squinting is a sign of ocular discomfort. Corneal ulceration, foreign material, uveitis, trauma, conjunctivitis, and glaucoma are among the possible causes.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

A painful eye should be examined promptly because some conditions can threaten vision or worsen rapidly.

Same-day evaluation is appropriate for persistent squinting, marked redness, cloudiness, trauma, unequal pupils, a protruding eye, sudden vision change, or severe discharge.

Corneal ulcer

A scratch, foreign body, feline herpesvirus, eyelid abnormality, or trauma can damage the corneal surface. Fluorescein stain helps detect many ulcers. Some ulcers can deepen quickly.

Uveitis and glaucoma

Inflammation inside the eye can cause pain, redness, a small pupil, or cloudiness. Glaucoma involves elevated intraocular pressure and can also be painful. These conditions require different treatment, so guessing from appearance is unsafe.

Why leftover eye drops are risky

Some steroid-containing drops can worsen corneal ulcers. Medication that was appropriate for a previous eye problem may be inappropriate for a new one.

What can I do before the visit?

Prevent rubbing if possible and gently wipe external discharge with saline or clean damp gauze. Do not put human “redness relief” drops, anesthetic drops, or unprescribed medication into the eye.
