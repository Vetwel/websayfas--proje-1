Cat straining to pee: Could it be a urinary blockage?

Repeated litter-box trips, straining, crying, blood in the urine, or passing only drops can be signs of lower urinary tract disease. Passing little or no urine can be an emergency.

Veterinarian reviewedEmergency awareness guideUpdated Aug 15, 2026
No urine = emergency.

Cornell describes urethral obstruction as an absolute emergency requiring immediate veterinary treatment. Male cats are at increased risk because of their narrower urethra.

If your cat is repeatedly trying to urinate and little or no urine is coming out, go to a veterinarian or emergency hospital now. Do not wait overnight to see whether the problem resolves.

What does a urinary blockage look like?

  • Frequent trips to the litter box
  • Straining or staying in the box for a long time
  • Crying or vocalizing while trying to urinate
  • Passing only a few drops or no urine
  • Blood in the urine
  • Licking the genital area repeatedly
  • Restlessness, hiding, vomiting, or increasing distress

Why can this happen?

Feline lower urinary tract signs can be associated with inflammation, urinary crystals or stones, urethral plugs, infection in some cats, and other bladder or urethral disorders. The same outward signs can have different causes, which is why testing matters.

What will the veterinarian do?

The immediate priority in an obstructed cat is relieving the blockage and stabilizing the patient. Depending on the case, evaluation may include a physical exam, bloodwork, urinalysis, imaging, urine culture, and monitoring of kidney values and electrolytes.

Do not try to squeeze the bladder or pass anything into the urethra at home. Urinary obstruction requires professional veterinary treatment.

Can stress play a role?

Stress and environmental changes can be associated with feline lower urinary tract signs, especially in cats with idiopathic cystitis. Your veterinarian may discuss hydration, litter-box management, diet, environmental enrichment, and other preventive strategies after the emergency is addressed.
