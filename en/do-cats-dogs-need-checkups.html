Does my cat or dog really need regular checkups?

Preventive visits are not simply a vaccine appointment or an annual blood panel. They combine history, physical examination, risk assessment, life-stage planning, and targeted testing.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

A pet can look healthy while early dental, kidney, endocrine, weight, blood-pressure, or mobility problems are developing. Trends over time are valuable.

How often?

Healthy young and middle-aged pets commonly receive at least annual preventive evaluation. Senior animals often benefit from examination at least every six months, and chronic disease may require more frequent follow-up. Individual risk always matters.

What does the physical exam include?

Weight, body and muscle condition, teeth and gums, eyes, ears, skin, heart and lungs, abdomen, lymph nodes, joints, gait, pain, behavior, and reproductive status may all be assessed. The history adds appetite, thirst, elimination, medications, diet, lifestyle, and home observations.

Does every pet need every test every year?

No. CBC, chemistry, urinalysis, thyroid testing, blood pressure, parasite testing, heartworm/vector-borne screening, and imaging are selected according to age, species, geography, lifestyle, history, and examination findings. More testing is not automatically better testing.

Vaccines are only one part

Vaccination plans are individualized by disease risk and product schedule. Dental care, nutrition, parasite control, weight, behavior, and home safety are also preventive medicine.
