Does a cat that never goes outside still need a rabies vaccine?

Indoor living lowers exposure risk but does not make it zero. Bats can enter homes, doors and windows can fail, and cats can escape unexpectedly. Rabies is also a fatal zoonotic disease with public-health implications.

Veterinarian reviewedU.S. contextUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Rabies vaccination is considered a core feline vaccine. In the United States, legal requirements vary by state and local jurisdiction, so follow your veterinarian and local public-health rules.

Why vaccinate an indoor cat?

Rabies exposure does not require routine outdoor roaming. Bat encounters inside homes are an important real-world scenario. Vaccination also affects how public-health authorities manage a cat after potential exposure or after a bite incident.

Is rabies vaccination risk-free?

No medical intervention is completely risk-free. Most vaccine reactions are mild and temporary; serious reactions are uncommon. The decision balances the consequences of rabies, exposure possibility, legal requirements, and the individual cat's medical history.

What if my cat had a previous vaccine reaction?

Discuss the exact reaction with your veterinarian before the next dose. Product choice, timing, observation, and whether a medical exemption pathway exists depend on local law and the individual case. Do not simply allow the vaccine to lapse without professional guidance.

What schedule should I follow?

Licensed products and legal rules differ. Your veterinarian will choose an age-appropriate vaccine and revaccination interval based on the product label, health status, and applicable regulations.

Legal note: Rabies laws are jurisdiction-specific and can change. This page does not replace current state or local requirements.
