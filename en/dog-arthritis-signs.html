Dog arthritis: what are the early signs?

Dogs with osteoarthritis do not always cry or limp dramatically. Slower movement, stiffness after rest, reluctance on stairs and subtle behavior changes may be early signs of chronic pain.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
Quick answer

A dog that is slower to rise, avoids jumping, hesitates on stairs or becomes less willing to walk may be painful even without obvious limping.

Common arthritis signs

  • Stiffness after sleep or rest
  • Slower rising or lying down
  • Reluctance to jump into the car or onto furniture
  • Difficulty with stairs or slippery floors
  • Shorter walks or lagging behind
  • Licking over a painful joint
  • Irritability or reduced social interaction

How is arthritis diagnosed?

A veterinarian combines history, gait observation, orthopedic examination and sometimes radiographs. Other causes of lameness or weakness may need to be ruled out, especially when signs appear suddenly or affect one limb.

What helps?

Management is usually multimodal. Weight control, appropriate exercise, rehabilitation, environmental modifications and veterinarian-prescribed pain management can all contribute. The plan changes with the dog's age, other diseases and response to treatment.

Do not give human pain medications such as ibuprofen or naproxen unless specifically directed by a veterinarian; some can be highly toxic to dogs.
