Dog diarrhea: what causes it and when should you worry?

Dietary indiscretion is common, but parasites, infection, hemorrhagic diarrhea, pancreatitis, medication effects, food-responsive disease, and systemic illness can produce similar stool changes.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

The dog’s overall condition matters as much as the stool. A bright adult dog with one soft stool is different from a weak puppy with repeated bloody diarrhea.

Seek prompt care for large amounts of blood, black/tarry stool, repeated vomiting, collapse, dehydration, severe abdominal pain, fever, or diarrhea in an unvaccinated puppy.

Common triggers

Sudden diet change, rich food, garbage, parasites, stress, bacterial or viral disease, medication effects, and chronic intestinal disorders are common possibilities. Parvovirus is an important consideration in inadequately vaccinated puppies.

What can owners do for a mild episode?

Keep fresh water available and avoid repeated rich treats or abrupt food changes. A veterinarian may recommend a highly digestible diet or other supportive care based on the dog’s age and history. Do not give human antidiarrheal medication without professional advice.

Why stool testing matters

Parasites are not always visible to the naked eye. Fecal testing can identify common parasites and guide targeted treatment. Recurrent diarrhea may require broader diagnostic testing.

When it becomes chronic

Diarrhea lasting or recurring over weeks, especially with weight loss, appetite change, poor body condition, or vomiting, deserves investigation for chronic enteropathy, pancreatic disease, endocrine disease, and other causes.
