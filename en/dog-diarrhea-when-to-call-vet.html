Dog diarrhea: When should you call the vet?

Loose stool can follow a diet change or dietary indiscretion, but it can also accompany parasites, infection, inflammation, toxins, or other disease.

Veterinarian reviewedDigestive health guideUpdated Aug 15, 2026
Watch the whole dog, not just the stool.

Energy level, appetite, vomiting, hydration, stool color, duration, age, and underlying medical conditions all change how urgent diarrhea may be.

Contact a veterinarian promptly if diarrhea is accompanied by repeated vomiting, marked lethargy, refusal to eat, black/tarry stool, significant blood, abdominal pain, or rapid decline. Puppies, seniors, and medically fragile dogs may need earlier care.

Why do dogs get diarrhea?

Common short-term triggers include eating something unusual, switching foods too quickly, stress, parasites, or mild gastrointestinal upset. More serious causes can include infection, foreign material, inflammatory disease, toxins, pancreatic disease, endocrine disease, and other systemic illness.

How long is too long?

Cornell advises veterinary care if diarrhea does not resolve within roughly 48–72 hours, or sooner if a dog stops eating, becomes lethargic, vomits, or passes black/tarry stool. Persistent fluid loss can contribute to dehydration and electrolyte imbalance.

Should I change my dog’s food right away?

Abrupt diet changes can themselves trigger loose stool. If diarrhea started after a recent food switch, tell your veterinarian what changed and when. Repeatedly changing foods during an active episode can make it harder to identify the trigger.

What information helps the veterinarian?

  • How long the diarrhea has been present
  • How many bowel movements occur per day
  • Whether there is blood, mucus, or black stool
  • Vomiting, appetite, thirst, and energy changes
  • Recent food changes, table scraps, garbage, travel, boarding, or stress
  • Current medications and parasite prevention
Bring a fresh stool sample if your clinic asks for one. Fecal testing can help identify parasites and other causes when appropriate.
