Why is my dog licking his paws?

Occasional grooming is normal. Persistent licking, chewing, redness, odor, staining, swelling, or limping suggests irritation, infection, allergy, injury, or pain.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

One painful paw and four itchy paws point toward different possibilities. Distribution is a useful diagnostic clue.

Allergy is common—but not the only cause

Environmental allergy and food allergy can involve the paws, but yeast or bacterial infection may develop secondarily. Fleas and mites, although less likely to affect only the paws, may contribute to generalized itch.

One paw deserves a close look

A grass awn, splinter, torn nail, cut, burn, interdigital cyst-like lesion, insect sting, or orthopedic pain can cause focused licking. Sudden limping or swelling should be assessed promptly.

What does the veterinarian check?

The workup may include a detailed paw and nail examination, skin cytology, parasite testing, and evaluation for allergy or pain. Treating a secondary infection without addressing the trigger often leads to recurrence.

What can owners do?

Rinse obvious irritants with plain water, dry the paws well, keep parasite prevention current, and stop access to open wounds with veterinarian-approved methods. Avoid repeated peroxide, alcohol, essential oils, or unprescribed creams, which can irritate skin or be unsafe if licked.

This simple observation can help separate local injury from generalized itch.
