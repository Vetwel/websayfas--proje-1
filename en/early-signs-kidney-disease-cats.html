What are the early signs of kidney disease in cats?

Chronic kidney disease can progress quietly. Subtle changes in thirst, urination, body weight, appetite, and muscle condition may appear before a cat looks obviously ill.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Senior-cat screening matters because bloodwork, urinalysis, blood pressure, body weight, and trends over time can reveal problems earlier than appearance alone.

Early changes owners may notice

  • Drinking more water
  • Larger or more frequent urine clumps
  • Gradual weight or muscle loss
  • Reduced appetite or food preferences changing
  • Intermittent nausea or vomiting
  • Lower activity or less grooming

How kidney disease is evaluated

Diagnosis and staging can involve serum creatinine, SDMA, urine concentration, urine protein assessment, blood pressure, electrolytes, imaging, and evaluation for other diseases. No single value should be interpreted in isolation.

Why blood pressure and urine matter

High blood pressure and protein loss through the kidneys can affect prognosis and treatment decisions. Urinalysis also helps determine whether the kidneys are concentrating urine appropriately and whether infection or other urinary disease is present.

Can CKD be cured?

Most chronic kidney disease is managed rather than cured. Treatment is individualized and may include therapeutic renal nutrition, attention to hydration, control of nausea or blood pressure, correction of electrolyte abnormalities, and other measures based on the cat’s stage and complications. The goal is to support quality of life and slow progression where possible.

Track trends

Weight, water intake, appetite, and urine changes are useful to record between visits.
