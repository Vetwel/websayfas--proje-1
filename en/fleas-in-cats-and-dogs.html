How can I tell if my cat or dog has fleas?

Adult fleas can be hard to spot. Flea dirt, intense itching, hair loss near the tail base, scabs, or recurrent skin infection may be the first clues.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Most of the flea life cycle occurs off the pet, so effective control usually requires treating every appropriate pet and managing the environment over time.

What is flea dirt?

Flea dirt is digested blood passed by adult fleas. It looks like black pepper-like specks. When placed on a damp white paper towel, it may create a reddish-brown halo as the blood pigment dissolves.

Why one flea can matter

Pets with flea-allergy dermatitis can itch severely from very few bites. Fleas can also transmit Dipylidium tapeworm and, in heavy infestations, contribute to anemia—especially in kittens and puppies.

Use species-appropriate prevention

Products differ in active ingredient, age limit, weight range, and species safety. Some dog flea products containing permethrin can be dangerously toxic to cats. Never use a dog product on a cat unless the label specifically states it is safe.

Why infestations take time to clear

Eggs, larvae, and pupae in the environment continue to mature after treatment begins. Vacuuming, washing bedding, and consistent veterinary-recommended prevention help break the life cycle. Stopping after the first few flea-free days can allow the infestation to rebound.
