Why is my cat losing hair?

Normal shedding produces loose hair, but visible bald spots, broken hairs, inflamed skin, crusts, or intense grooming suggest a medical or behavioral problem.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Alopecia describes hair loss; it does not identify the cause. The skin, grooming behavior, parasite risk, and overall health all need to be considered.

Common causes

Fleas, mites, ringworm, bacterial or yeast infection, allergic disease, excessive grooming, pain, and less commonly hormonal or systemic illnesses can lead to hair loss. Some cats lick the hair away without owners ever seeing them groom excessively.

Pattern matters

Symmetrical hair loss on the belly or inner thighs may suggest overgrooming, while circular scaly areas can raise concern for dermatophytosis. Hair loss near the tail base is common with flea allergy. These patterns are clues, not diagnoses.

How veterinarians investigate

Testing may include flea-comb examination, skin scraping, cytology, fungal testing, or a therapeutic trial. If the skin looks normal but grooming is excessive, pain and medical disease should be considered before assuming the problem is purely behavioral.

When should I make an appointment?

Arrange evaluation for rapidly expanding bald areas, open sores, severe itching, odor, crusting, weight loss, appetite change, or lesions that may be contagious to people or other animals. Avoid applying human antifungal or steroid creams without veterinary guidance.
