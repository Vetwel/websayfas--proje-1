Kidney disease in cats: What are the early signs?

Chronic kidney disease is common in older cats and may be subtle early on. Increased drinking and urination, weight loss, appetite changes, and poor coat quality can develop as kidney function declines.

Veterinarian reviewedSenior-cat health guideUpdated Aug 15, 2026
Early disease may be quiet.

Cornell notes that cats with early CKD can show no obvious signs. Routine senior-cat exams, bloodwork, urinalysis, blood pressure, and trend monitoring can help identify problems sooner.

What do healthy kidneys do?

Kidneys filter waste from the blood, help control water and electrolyte balance, contribute to blood-pressure regulation, and support red-blood-cell production. Chronic kidney disease means this function is persistently reduced over time.

What signs should cat owners watch for?

  • Drinking more water
  • Urinating larger volumes or more often
  • Weight loss
  • Reduced appetite
  • Lethargy or weakness
  • Vomiting or nausea
  • Poor coat quality or reduced grooming
  • Bad breath in some advanced cases

Why do cats drink more with kidney disease?

As kidneys lose the ability to concentrate urine effectively, more water is lost in the urine. Cats may compensate by drinking more. Increased thirst is not specific to kidney disease, however; diabetes, hyperthyroidism, medications, and other conditions can also cause it.

How is CKD diagnosed?

Veterinarians interpret several pieces together rather than relying on one number. Blood tests such as creatinine, BUN, and SDMA, urine concentration and protein, blood pressure, hydration status, body weight, and imaging may all contribute to the assessment.

Do not diagnose kidney disease from thirst alone. A change in drinking or urination is a reason to schedule an exam and testing.

What does treatment usually focus on?

Management depends on stage and the individual cat. Veterinary plans may include a therapeutic renal diet, hydration support, blood-pressure management, phosphorus control, treatment of nausea or anemia, and regular monitoring.
