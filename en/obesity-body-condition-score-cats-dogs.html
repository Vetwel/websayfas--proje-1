Is my cat or dog overweight?

A scale gives body weight, but body condition score (BCS) helps determine whether that weight represents appropriate fat stores. Muscle condition should be assessed separately.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Healthy weight management is not about rapid restriction. It combines an accurate calorie plan, complete nutrition, activity, monitoring, and adjustment over time.

How body condition scoring works

Common 9-point BCS systems assess rib coverage, waist, and abdominal tuck. In an ideal pet, ribs are usually easy to feel under a thin fat layer and a waist is visible from above. Breed and coat can make visual judgment unreliable, so hands-on assessment matters.

Why obesity matters

Excess body fat is associated with reduced mobility and quality of life and can complicate arthritis, diabetes, respiratory disease, anesthesia, and other conditions. It is a chronic health problem rather than a cosmetic issue.

Measure food, treats, and extras

Calories from treats, table food, chews, and supplements count. Measuring cups can be imprecise; weighing food in grams improves consistency. The label feeding guide is a starting point, not a personalized prescription.

Protect muscle during weight loss

Safe programs aim to reduce fat while preserving lean tissue and meeting nutrient needs. Rapid or severe calorie restriction is especially risky in cats because of hepatic lipidosis. Regular weigh-ins and BCS/MCS reassessment guide adjustments.
