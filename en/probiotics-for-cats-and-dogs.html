Probiotics for cats and dogs: what do they actually do?

Probiotics are live microorganisms intended to provide a health benefit when given in adequate amounts. In veterinary medicine, effects are strain-specific and should not be generalized to every product.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

“Contains probiotics” is not enough information. Species, strain, viable dose, stability, clinical evidence, and the pet’s condition all matter.

Where might probiotics be useful?

Evidence is most practical in selected gastrointestinal situations, including some forms of acute diarrhea, antibiotic-associated microbiome disruption, and supportive management of certain chronic intestinal disorders. Benefits are not guaranteed and vary by formulation.

Probiotic, prebiotic, and synbiotic

A probiotic contains live microorganisms. A prebiotic is a substrate used by beneficial microorganisms. A synbiotic combines the two. These terms describe different product concepts and should not be used interchangeably.

What should be on the label?

Look for clearly identified microorganisms, ideally to the strain level; viable counts through the end of shelf life; storage instructions; and manufacturer quality information. A very high CFU number alone does not prove that a product is clinically effective.

When should you call the veterinarian instead?

Bloody diarrhea, repeated vomiting, dehydration, severe lethargy, weight loss, fever, abdominal pain, or persistent gastrointestinal signs need diagnostic evaluation rather than supplement-only management. Puppies, kittens, and immunocompromised pets deserve additional caution.
