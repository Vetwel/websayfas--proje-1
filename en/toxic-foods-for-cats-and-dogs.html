Which foods are toxic to cats and dogs?

Common kitchen foods can cause neurologic, cardiac, gastrointestinal, blood-cell, liver, or kidney injury in pets. Risk depends on the ingredient, amount, body weight, species, and individual sensitivity.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

If a potentially toxic food was eaten, call a veterinarian or animal poison-control service before waiting for symptoms or trying to induce vomiting.

Important foods to keep away from pets

  • Chocolate and cocoa
  • Xylitol-containing gum, candy, baked goods, or dental products
  • Grapes and raisins
  • Onions, garlic, chives, and related Allium plants
  • Alcohol and raw yeast dough
  • Caffeine-containing products
  • Macadamia nuts in dogs
  • Moldy foods and some high-fat table scraps

Why “a small amount” is not always reassuring

Toxic dose varies widely. Dark chocolate contains more methylxanthine than milk chocolate. Xylitol can cause severe hypoglycemia in dogs at relatively small doses. Grape and raisin toxicity is unpredictable enough that no reliable safe dose can be assumed.

What information should you have ready?

Product name, ingredient list, amount eaten, time of exposure, and pet weight help guide risk assessment. Keep packaging and do not discard vomited material if identification is uncertain.

Do not use random home antidotes

Salt, mustard, oil, milk, and other internet remedies can cause additional harm. Hydrogen peroxide is not appropriate for every species or exposure and should not be used unless a veterinary professional specifically directs it.
