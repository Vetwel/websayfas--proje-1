Why does my dog eat poop?

Coprophagia is unpleasant to people but relatively common in dogs. It is often behavioral rather than proof of a nutritional deficiency.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

The most reliable management starts with preventing access and rewarding an alternative behavior—not punishment after the dog has already eaten stool.

Why dogs may do it

Puppy exploration, learned behavior, scavenging, attention, anxiety, competition, or simply access can maintain coprophagia. Mother dogs normally ingest puppy feces during early nursing, so stool-eating behavior is not biologically foreign to the species.

When should medical causes be considered?

A sudden new behavior combined with weight loss, excessive hunger, chronic diarrhea, poor body condition, or increased thirst deserves veterinary evaluation for malabsorption, parasites, endocrine disease, or other illness.

Practical management

Pick up stool immediately, supervise outdoor elimination, teach a strong recall or “leave it,” and reward the dog for moving away from feces. Prevent access to cat litter boxes and wildlife feces when possible.

What about deterrent powders?

Products intended to make stool less appealing have inconsistent results because motivation varies. They work best, if at all, as an adjunct to environmental control and training rather than as the core solution.

Parasite prevention still matters

Eating feces can increase exposure to some intestinal parasites and pathogens.
