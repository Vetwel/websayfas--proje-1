Why is my cat drinking so much water?

A noticeable increase in thirst—especially together with larger urine clumps, weight loss or appetite change—can be an early sign of disease and deserves attention.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
Quick answer

Common medical causes include chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus and hyperthyroidism. Diet and weather can affect water intake too, but persistent change should be investigated.

What counts as “drinking more”?

Absolute volume is difficult to judge in multi-cat homes, so look for trends: more frequent drinking, emptying bowls faster, drinking from unusual places, larger litter clumps or urinating more often.

Common causes

Kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, hyperthyroidism and some medications are important causes. Less common endocrine, liver or electrolyte problems may also increase thirst and urination.

What should I track?

  • Body weight
  • Appetite
  • Urine-clump size and frequency
  • Vomiting or diarrhea
  • Activity level
  • Medication changes

What tests may be recommended?

Bloodwork and urinalysis are common starting points. Depending on age and findings, blood pressure, thyroid testing, urine culture or imaging may be appropriate.
