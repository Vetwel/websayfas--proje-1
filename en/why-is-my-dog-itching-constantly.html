Why is my dog itching constantly?

Chronic scratching, licking, rubbing, or chewing is a sign that something is irritating the skin. The cause may be allergic, parasitic, infectious, or occasionally related to pain or another disease.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
The key takeaway

Treating the itch alone may bring temporary relief, but long-term control usually depends on identifying the underlying trigger and any secondary infection.

Common causes of itching

Flea allergy, environmental allergies, food allergy, mites, bacterial or yeast skin infection, and contact irritation are frequent causes. More than one problem can be present at the same time—for example, an allergic dog may develop a secondary skin infection that greatly increases itching.

Look at the pattern

Paw licking, recurrent ear infections, belly or armpit redness, face rubbing, seasonal flares, hair loss, odor, scabs, or hot spots can help guide the workup. Fleas may be difficult to see, so the absence of visible fleas does not completely rule out flea-associated disease.

How is the cause diagnosed?

Your veterinarian may use skin cytology, parasite testing, response to flea control, diet trials, or other testing depending on the history and examination. Allergy testing is not a stand-alone diagnosis for every itchy dog; it is used in specific clinical contexts.

What can owners do?

Use veterinarian-recommended parasite prevention, avoid unapproved human creams or medications, follow bathing instructions carefully, and complete treatment for secondary infections. Seek faster care if there are open wounds, severe pain, facial swelling, breathing difficulty, or rapidly worsening skin disease.
