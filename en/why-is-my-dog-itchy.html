Why is my dog so itchy?

Repeated scratching, paw licking, rubbing, redness and recurrent ear problems are symptoms—not a diagnosis. Fleas, skin infection, environmental allergy, food reactions and several other problems can look similar.

Veterinarian reviewedUpdated Aug 20, 2026
Quick answer

If itching is frequent, keeps returning, disturbs sleep, causes hair loss or leads to red, wet or painful skin, a veterinary exam is the best next step.

Seek veterinary care promptly if the skin is painful, oozing, badly inflamed, your dog is injuring itself, or there is facial swelling or trouble breathing.

What are the most common reasons dogs itch?

Common causes include fleas and other parasites, bacterial or yeast infection, environmental allergy, food-related reactions and contact irritation. Dogs can have more than one problem at the same time—for example, allergic skin disease complicated by a secondary yeast or bacterial infection.

What does allergy-related itching look like?

Allergic dogs often lick or chew the paws and scratch the face, ears, armpits, belly or groin. Recurrent ear infections are common. Some dogs flare seasonally while others itch year-round, so the timing and body distribution are useful clues.

Why does my dog keep licking its paws?

Paw licking can reflect itch, pain, irritation, infection or behavior. Redness between the toes, odor, discharge, swelling or repeated chewing should not simply be covered up. Read our focused guide on why dogs lick their paws.

What will my veterinarian check?

Your veterinarian may examine the skin and ears, look for parasites, perform skin or ear cytology, review flea prevention, diet and seasonality, and decide whether an elimination diet or allergy workup is appropriate. Treatment should address both the itch and the underlying trigger.

Important: Stopping the itch and treating the cause are not always the same thing. Recurring disease often needs a long-term plan that combines trigger control, treatment of secondary infection, skin-barrier support and follow-up.

What can I do while waiting for an appointment?

  • Prevent excessive self-trauma if possible.
  • Keep veterinarian-recommended flea prevention current.
  • Avoid changing food, shampoo and supplements all at once.
  • Photograph flare-ups and note seasonality.
  • Track paw licking, ear problems and new foods or treats.

Questions dog owners often ask

Can a dog have allergies without sneezing?

Yes. In dogs, allergies often show up primarily as itchy skin, paw licking and recurrent ear disease rather than human-style hay-fever symptoms.

Does itchy skin always mean food allergy?

No. Food reactions are only one differential diagnosis. Fleas, environmental allergy and infection are also common, and an elimination diet should be structured carefully if your veterinarian recommends one.
