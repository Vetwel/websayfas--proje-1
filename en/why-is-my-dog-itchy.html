Why is my dog so itchy?

Repeated scratching, paw licking, rubbing, redness, and recurrent ear problems can have many causes. The pattern matters because “itchy skin” is a symptom, not a diagnosis.

Veterinarian reviewedSymptom-first SEO guideUpdated Aug 15, 2026
Do not assume it is “just allergies.”

Parasites, skin infections, food reactions, environmental allergies, and other conditions can look similar. A veterinary exam helps separate them.

Seek veterinary care promptly if the skin is painful, oozing, badly inflamed, or your dog is injuring itself. Severe itching can quickly lead to skin trauma and secondary infection.

What are the most common reasons dogs itch?

Common causes include fleas and other parasites, bacterial or yeast infections, environmental allergies, food-related reactions, contact irritation, and less commonly other medical problems. More than one cause can be present at the same time.

What does allergy-related itching look like?

Dogs with allergic skin disease may lick or chew their paws, scratch the face, ears, armpits, belly, or groin, and develop recurrent ear or skin infections. Some dogs have seasonal patterns; others itch year-round.

Why does my dog keep licking its paws?

Paw licking may reflect itch, pain, irritation, infection, or behavior. Redness between the toes, odor, discharge, swelling, or repeated chewing deserves a closer look rather than simply covering the paws.

What will my veterinarian check?

Your veterinarian may examine the skin and ears, check for parasites, perform cytology or other tests for infection, review diet and seasonality, and assess whether an allergy workup or elimination diet is appropriate. Treatment depends on the cause.

Important: Stopping the itch and treating the cause are not always the same thing. Dogs with recurring problems often need a long-term plan that addresses triggers, secondary infections, skin-barrier care, and follow-up.

What can I do while waiting for an appointment?

  • Prevent excessive self-trauma if possible.
  • Keep flea prevention current as recommended by your veterinarian.
  • Avoid adding multiple new shampoos, foods, or supplements at once.
  • Take photos of flare-ups and note when they happen.
  • Track ear problems, paw licking, seasonality, and diet changes.
